1. System Architecture Overview

Component-Based Movement

All movement mechanics are built through modular Unreal Engine components.

Key components include:

  • Slide Component – handles ground-slide, slide-hop, capsule scaling, friction overrides.

  • Wall Run Component – manages wall detection, surface checks, directional calculations, and wall-run physics.

  • Grapple Component – controls grapple firing, hit detection, tension forces, pull behavior, and release logic.

  • Jump Component – extends regular jumps with slide-jumps, wall-jumps, and momentum-preserving boosts.

  • Movement State Handler – central controller managing transitions between movement states (Running → Sliding → Wallrunning → Grappling, etc.)

This modular approach keeps logic isolated, makes debugging easier, and allows individual mechanics to be iterated or replaced without affecting the full movement system.

2. Slide System

Functionality

The slide system enables:

  • Standard ground slide—Linear force applied along the ground surface’s tangent, and also sets the player's location (only the Z axis) on the predicted ground location to make the player stick to the ground when sliding.

  • Slide-hop (jumping mid-slide to convert horizontal velocity into a boost). (If hop is triggered when going up a slope, additional force is applied along the ground surface normal to give extra height to the jump).

  • Momentum preservation when transitioning out of the slide into wallruns or grapple.

Technical Breakdown

  • Activation Conditions: Triggered when sprinting above a minimum velocity and the player presses Crouch.

  • Capsule Scaling: Capsule height smoothly lerps to a lower profile to avoid clipping and maintain readability.

  • Friction Override: Ground friction is reduced to allow long slides and speed maintenance.

  • Slide Direction Logic: The slide direction is determined by the player’s input; if no input is provided, the player's forward vector is used as the direction, allowing momentum-based steering.

  • Slide-Hop Calculation: When the player jumps during a slide:

    • Vertical boost is only partial to avoid floatiness.

    • Horizontal velocity is preserved and increased slightly.

    Adds a skill-based timing mechanic similar to bunny-hopping.

This system encourages chaining slides to maintain maximum ground speed.

3. Wall Run System

Functionality

The player can run horizontally or vertically on walls, transition between walls, and preserve speed.

Technical Breakdown

  • Wall Detection: Forward and side line traces identify valid wall surfaces and the angle of player with respect to the wall.

    Surfaces are validated using:

    • Surface normal vs player camera’s forward vector

    • Wall surface curvature

    • Player current velocity

  • Direction Calculation: Wall run direction = $\text{Cross}(\text{WallNormal}, \text{WorldUp})$ with left/right validation. If the direction opposes player momentum, the system flips it.

  • Physics & Velocity: Custom forces simulate “stickiness” keeping player attached without feeling magnetic. Vertical gravity is reduced. Horizontal velocity is interpolated toward a target wall-run speed which depends on the player’s initial velocity.

  • Visual Feedback: Player camera tilts slightly based on wall-run direction, and weapon animation changes by modifying shoulder bone rotation and position for feedback.

This system rewards maintaining angle, speed, and spatial awareness.

4. Grapple System

Functionality

A fast, skill-based grapple designed to maintain and amplify momentum, similar to Titanfall 2.

Technical Breakdown

  • Grapple Firing: A sphere trace from the camera center checks for valid grapple surfaces. The location is stored and used as a buffer if there is no other valid surface, and the player's aim is not far away from the buffer point. Hook attaches to world geometry or enemies and moves if the enemies move, as it becomes a child of the attached actor.

  • Force Application: Grapple behavior uses a spring-like force model:

    • Pull strength increases as the rope tightens.

    • Force is applied along the grapple point from the player's location, and one force is applied along the camera’s forward direction.

    • Additional force is applied towards the start of the grapple along the player’s up vector to give him some height and provide a clean arc motion whenever the player grapples to the ground.

    • Grapple is detached if the player exceeds the initial grapple tangent in order to prevent high centrifugal force and make the grapple behave like a catapult. Maximum speed cap prevents uncontrollable launches.

  • Momentum Integration: Grapple releases automatically when the player reaches max swing angle or manually releases.


    Upon release:

    • Player inherits swing velocity.

    • Vertical component is boosted depending on swing arc.

    Allows chaining into quick ground slam, wallruns, or slide hop.

This creates a highly expressive traversal tool.

5. Ground Slam System

Functionality

A fast downward slam that can be chained after jumps or grapple swings.

Technical Breakdown

  • Vertical velocity is overwritten with a strong downward impulse.

  • Upon impact:

    • Velocity is converted into a forward burst if player holds movement input.

    • Slam can transition immediately into a slide for rapid traversal.

This adds dramatic vertical control and is great for maintaining flow after high jumps, and to reset the grapple cooldown.

6. Jump System

Functionality

Multiple jump types built around momentum preservation.

Jump Types

  • Normal JumpStandard vertical impulse.

  • Slide JumpConverts slide velocity into stronger forward jump.

  • Wall JumpUses wall normal + view direction to push player away while preserving speed.

  • Grapple JumpAdditional forward velocity is added along camera's forward direction to give the feel of a catapult.

Technical Breakdown

Jump calculations adapt based on:

  • Current state (sliding, wallrunning, airborne)

  • Ground check status

  • Velocity vectors

  • Player camera orientation

This makes jumping part of the player's decision-making and expression, not just a binary action.

7. Movement State Handler

Purpose

The Movement State Handler acts as the central controller for:

  • State transitions

  • Prioritizing movement mechanics

  • Managing timers, cooldowns, and interrupt rules

Examples:

  • Slide cannot override grapple unless forced.

  • Wallrun has priority over normal airborne state.

  • Jump modifies behavior depending on active state.

This ensures predictable, consistent behavior across complex transitions.

8. Momentum Philosophy

All mechanics share a core design rule:

“Momentum is survival; therefore should be rewarded.”

This is achieved by:

  • Never zeroing velocity unless absolutely necessary.

  • Using additive or redirected forces instead of hard-set velocities.

  • Allowing boosts when chaining multiple movement styles.

  • Ensuring angles, timing, and skill impact speed gains.

The result is a high-expressiveness movement system where mastery leads to dramatically higher speed and control.
